# Images

Images is where you make a picture — a social card, an ad, a banner, a diagram. Pick a size, name it, then describe the image in plain words. It appears as you go.

Unlike the writing apps, an image is **born at a size**. A document can grow; a story card is 1080×1920 or it isn't a story card. So creating one asks for its dimensions first.

## Sizes

Preset stages, or your own:

**Square** · **Story** · **Wide** · **Ad** · **Portrait** · **Banner** · **Image** · **Free**

Pick **Free** and you set the width and height yourself.

## The grammar of an image

An image is a **stack of layers on an artboard**. That's the third shape the workspace composes: a deck is a sequence of pages, a document and a post flow, and an image is layers on a fixed canvas.

The rail on the left is the stack. Layers sit in front of or behind one another, and selecting one gives you a bounding box with handles — drag to position, resize, reorder.

## Blocks

The same vocabulary as the rest of the workspace, placed on an artboard rather than in a flow:

Heading · Text · Callout · Quote · Divider · Button · Table · Metrics · Chart · Figure · Gallery

A Chart is drawn from a CSV in your workspace, live. Figures cite real workspace files rather than pasting copies — so an image made from a file in your substrate stays connected to it.

## The bound lane

The right-hand column is a chat lane bound to the image you have open, and the agent beside you is **Designer**, whose whole job is making images.

Describe what you want and it composes onto the canvas — it has the same block vocabulary you do, and its work lands as attributed revisions like yours.

## Design systems

An image can wear your **design system**, the same one your decks and posts use — so a set of cards comes out looking like one family rather than eight separate attempts.

## File operations

The open image carries the standard verbs: rename, move, duplicate, copy link, move to trash. They behave identically to the same verbs on the [Files](files.md) surface.

## Export

An image leaves as a raster file you can download.
