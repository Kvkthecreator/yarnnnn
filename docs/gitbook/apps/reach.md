# Reach

Reach is the boundary. It answers one question: **what does this workspace touch that isn't this workspace** — what's connected, what's about to go out, and what already crossed.

Everything else in the desk is about work inside the commons. Reach is the door.

## Three panes

**Connected** — the platforms you've connected, and what each one actually reads and writes. Not a list of logos: each row says what that connection *does*, derived from the machinery that enacts it rather than from a description someone wrote once.

**Leaving** — anything about to cross the boundary, waiting on you. A publish, a send, a post. Consequential acts surface here before they bind, and you execute or reject them. This is where the workspace's proposals live.

**Crossed** — what has already gone out, and when.

## Connections are yours, not the workspace's

This is the part worth reading twice.

**A connection is held under your account, keyed to you as a person.** Other members of the same workspace hold and see their own. Connecting Slack doesn't give the workspace Slack — it gives *your* work in that workspace a path through *your* Slack.

**Agents are never handed your credential.** An agent-shaped caller asking for one is refused outright. When an agent's work needs to cross the boundary, it surfaces on Leaving as something for you to click — your reach, your act, your name on it.

The practical consequence: if a teammate can't see the connection you set up, that's not a bug. It's the boundary working.

## What you can connect

**Slack** · **Notion** · **GitHub** · **WordPress**

Slack, Notion and GitHub are inbound — the workspace reads through them. **WordPress is outbound**: it's the path a [Blogger](blogger.md) post takes to get published.

You can also attach an MCP connector, which appears here alongside the first-party connections.

> **Scheduled background pulling is not running yet.** The capture lane — the machinery that would sweep a connected platform on a cadence — is turned off. Connections work when you or your work reach through them; nothing pulls on its own yet.

## The queue moved here

If you're looking for the approval queue, it's the **Leaving** pane. Reach absorbed it: a proposal is a thing about to cross the boundary, so it belongs at the boundary rather than on a surface of its own.
