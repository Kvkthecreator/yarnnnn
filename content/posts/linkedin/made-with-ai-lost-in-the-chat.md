The real work happens with AI now — in a window only you can see.

And getting any of it in front of another person is still copy-paste, by hand, every time.

I want to describe the seam precisely, because it is easy to mistake for a minor annoyance.

You spend forty minutes with an assistant working out a plan. The plan is good. It exists as a long message about two-thirds of the way down a conversation that also contains three abandoned directions and a tangent about pricing.

A week later you need it. You remember having it. You do not remember which conversation, and search returns four near-identical threads.

Meanwhile the person the plan was for has never seen it — because getting it to them means you deciding to ferry it. Opening a doc, pasting, cleaning up the formatting, adding the two sentences of context that make it legible to someone who was not in the conversation.

The work is done. The delivery is entirely manual, and it is on you every single time.

I used to think a shared doc fixed this. It does not, and the reason decides what an actual fix looks like.

A shared doc solves location. Everyone can now reach the thing. But watch what happens next: your colleague opens it, feeds it to their own assistant, asks for a tightening, pastes the result back. Now the doc has two authors and one of them is a model, and nothing in the doc records that.

Location was never the hard part. The hard part is that nothing remembers, so a person has to.
