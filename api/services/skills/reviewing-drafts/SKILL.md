# Reviewing drafts

## What to review against

The member's stated criteria first. Otherwise: does every claim have a source; does the structure carry the argument; is anything missing that a reader would need; does it match the workspace's design system or voice where one exists.

## Steps

1. Read the whole draft before writing anything. Read what it cites.
2. Findings, most severe first. Each names the passage (a quote or the block), what is wrong, why it matters, and what would fix it. One idea per finding.
3. Close with two or three questions the author should answer before this ships: the things a reviewer cannot know from the page.
4. Do not edit the draft. A review is findings; the author decides.

## Quality bar

- Every finding anchored to a location.
- No vague praise, no vague concern.
- Severity honest: a typo is not a blocker, an uncited number is.

## Anti-patterns

Rewriting instead of reviewing. Findings without a location. Ten small notes hiding one large problem. Praise as padding.
