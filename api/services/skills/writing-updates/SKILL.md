# Writing updates

## Pick the shape

- Status report: what happened, what is next, blockers.
- Progress / plans / problems: three headed lists, one screen.
- Leadership summary: decisions needed first, then context, one screen.
- Incident report: timeline, impact, cause, fix, follow-ups.
- FAQ: the questions people actually asked, answered plainly.
- Announcement: what changes, for whom, from when, what to do.

If the workspace already holds an update of the same kind, match its shape.

## Steps

1. Gather facts from the workspace: recent revisions in the folder, maintained files, run receipts. Every fact cited by path.
2. Draft to the shape. Lead with what the reader must decide or know.
3. Mark what you could not evidence as "(unconfirmed)" rather than dropping it or inventing it.
4. Land it beside the work it reports on, with `derived_from` naming its sources.

## Quality bar

- The reader can act after one read.
- Numbers cited. Dates absolute. No claim about a person's intent.
- No longer than the work it reports on deserves.

## Anti-patterns

Inventing progress. "Great progress this week." Burying the ask. Restating the plan instead of reporting against it.
