Persistent storage for AI work is table stakes now.

Several tools keep your files between sessions, organise them into folders, and search them by meaning. They do it well. If a tool cannot do this in 2026 it is behind.

The distinguishing word is not persistent. It is signed.

A persistence layer answers one question well: where did my work go? It cannot answer the three that bite on anything more than one person touches. Who made this. What was it made from. What did the other AI change.

Not because it is badly built — because it sees one principal and one vendor. It knows what it did. It has no view of your co-founder's edit in a different tool, or of what a different assistant changed on Thursday.

A folder tells you where the work is. It does not tell you whether to trust it.

There is a second gap worth naming. Most storage layers keep files. Very few keep the edge — the fact that this brief was made from that transcript and those three sources. Without it, every derived document arrives as an orphan, and deleting a source silently guts the things built on it.

This is also why "we keep version history" and "every version has an author" are not the same claim. Version control was invented for code, where the build is the oracle and attribution is a convenience. Knowledge work has no compiler. Nothing mechanically verifies that a brief still says what was decided. So attribution is not metadata attached to the history — it IS the history. Strip it out and what remains is a stack of undated plausible drafts.

Open the file you would least like to be wrong about and ask your tool: who last changed this, and what did they change?
