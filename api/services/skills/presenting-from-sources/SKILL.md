# Presenting from sources

Produce a DECK, a slide narrative a teammate could present, derived from the source. In Slides the deck is the bound artifact and the pane posture owns the slide and block grammar; these are the CONTENT constraints.

## Steps

1. Read the source fully. List the 5–10 claims it actually supports.
2. Shape the narrative: a title slide (the thesis), the claims one per slide, a closing slide (so-what, next steps).
3. Each slide: the TITLE is the claim, a sentence someone could disagree with, never a topic word. The body is the evidence: figures, quotes, comparisons from the source.
4. Numbers on a slide live in a CSV beside the deck, cited as a table or a chart, never typed into prose. Two copies of one number drift.

## Quality bar

- Every slide's claim traceable to the source. Where you extrapolate, mark the slide "(inferred)".
- 6–12 slides. A slide that carries no evidence is cut, not padded.
- The source's own numbers and phrases over paraphrase.

## Anti-patterns

Topic-word titles ("Market", "Team"). Wall-of-text slides. Agenda and divider filler. Claims the source never makes. Burying the thesis past slide one.
